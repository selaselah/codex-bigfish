# Rebuilt BigFish — editable model

`BigFish.blend` is the editable Blender model; `BigFish.glb` contains the portable rigged mesh. Unity uses `Assets/DesktopFish/Rebuilt/BigFish.prefab` and its two mesh assets. The Blender file opens in the generated idle pose. The GLB exports the bind/rest rig with neutral facial morph weights and no animation clips; Unity drives the gestures procedurally in `FishMotion.cs`.

This version authors mesh positions, topology, vertex colors, bones and six facial morphs in `Assets/DesktopFish/Editor/FishRebuiltModel.cs`. The generator does not read the previous 3D model, textures or skeleton. Sparse measurements from the local Ref review calibrate the new geometry through `FishModelGuides.cs`: visible head sections, headband edges, bow/shoe bounds and apron/strap anchors. The character appearance is also informed by the existing 2D pet and previous 3D screenshots; this is geometry provenance, not a claim of original character design.

Mesh density is recorded in `Assets/DesktopFish/Rebuilt/provenance.json`. The model uses two material slots, one generated 1024×1152 RGBA paint atlas, 51 bones and six expression morphs. Blender materials approximate the Unity toon shader rather than reproducing it exactly.

The current model has **19,986 triangles and 14,602 vertices** (17,610 triangles / 12,846 vertices in the body, 2,376 triangles / 1,756 vertices in the face), down from 53,052 triangles. The generator rejects a complete model above 20,000 triangles. Surface grids are reduced selectively; an offline index-only meshoptimizer pass then locks border seams, mixed weights, triangles crossing bone attachments and small independent ornaments. It retains UVs, colors, shading normals and weights of surviving vertices. Part counts in `work/rebuilt-budget.json` are authored counts before compaction/reduction, while its total is the actual final count. The reduction error limit is a local geometric tolerance, not a guarantee of identical silhouette or measured performance gain.

The additional 128 atlas rows contain newly drawn alpha scallops. Headband, apron edging and the two skirt ruffles use sparse curved sheets; the alpha channel resolves the outer edge. The original 1024×1024 paint pixels are unchanged and their UVs remap to the enlarged atlas. Unity clips both the main pass and outline at 0.5; Blender uses a matching binary alpha node and GLB exports `MASK`. No reference bitmap or mesh topology is embedded. The apron retains its whale and is reprojected onto the garment after its outline is resized, preserving the waist attachment.

Small independent buttons are generated with sparse grids and protected from body-scale simplification, which otherwise collapses their thickness. Gold cuff strips use 24 segments aligned with the sleeve’s 12-segment cross-section, keeping their 0.0036-unit width with fewer triangles and avoiding intersecting edges. Vertex alpha is fixed at one; texture alpha alone controls cutout coverage consistently in Unity and glTF.

The front-hair pass reads sparse local transverse landmarks for the cheek locks and fringe: roots, tips, edges and three interior depth samples per selected height. New interpolated surfaces use 2,560 triangles for the complete front hair. Their outlines follow those controls while preserving layered tips; their sampling is protected from the global simplifier. The backing crown is rounded inside those sheets. The paint remains the same authored atlas, so the highlights and parting lines still differ from Ref.

The existing material is two-sided, so most thin strips use one surface instead of nearly coincident front/back copies. This removes depth fighting. The two outer cheek locks retain a measured return with actual transverse depth, because that folded back surface is visible in oblique views. Unity lights the visible side consistently; Blender/GLB explicitly retain a double-sided MASK material. The remaining body uses a 0.003-unit simplification tolerance, with the full model still constrained to 20,000 triangles. This tolerance does not imply identical silhouette or an observed performance gain. Upper-crown joins, forehead clearance, rear hair and chest curls still require visual refinement; mapped landmarks are not a whole-model likeness score.

The chest-lock pass measures two distinct local reference families: the long inward S tip and shorter outward fork. Sparse cross-sections retain both sides of each folded section. New interpolated surfaces replace the earlier overlapping tube approximations; the final chest assembly has 1,056 triangles across eight surface components. Flattening the sections to their midline lost visible side volume and was rejected. The smaller fork uses fewer longitudinal rows than the long curl, keeping the whole export within the 20k budget without loosening the simplifier tolerance. The atlas pixels, all bones and the complete face/six morphs remain unchanged; hair UVs and head/secondary-bone blends are regenerated for the new surfaces.

Sparse bounds do not capture complete surfaces, and the 20k budget does not by itself establish visual completion. Historical notes below describe earlier iterations and their then-current counts.
